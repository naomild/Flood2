# Apple HIG — Writing

Source: Apple Human Interface Guidelines, *Writing* —
<https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/writing> (read 2026-11-26)

> "The words you choose within your app are an essential part of its user experience."

Quoted best-practice headings, with the key line from each:

- **"Determine your app's voice."** "Create a list of common terms, and reference that list
  to keep your language consistent."
- **"Match your tone to the context."** "Consider what people are doing while they're using
  your app — both in the physical world and within the app itself."
- **"Be clear."** "If you can use fewer words, do so. When in doubt, read your writing out loud."
- **"Write for everyone."** "Choose simple, plain language … avoiding jargon and gendered terminology."
- **"Be action oriented."** "When labeling buttons and links, it's almost always best to use a
  verb." "just saying 'Send' often works better than 'Let's do it!'" Avoid "Click here".
- **"Adopt capitalization rules that align with your app's style, then apply them consistently."**
- **"Use possessive pronouns sparingly."** "Avoid using *we* altogether" — "Unable to load
  content" beats "We're having trouble loading this content."
- **"Provide clear next steps on any blank screens."** "…don't show crucial information that
  could then disappear."
- **"Write clear error messages."** "display it as close to the problem as possible, avoid
  blame, and be clear about what someone can do to fix it." Skip "oops!".
- **"Keep settings labels clear and simple."** "Describe what it does when turned on".

## Flood word list (apply consistently)
Flood outlook · Power outage chance · Beach day · Low / Moderate / High / Very high ·
Today / Tomorrow · Updated 3:10 PM · Offline · Approve · Reject · Deploys to the live app.
Never: "safe", "we", "oops", "click here". Sentence case for headings and buttons.
